# CDL app: deeper moats that build speed can compound

*Oct 28, 2026 (PT). Read-only research. [E] = estimate and [J] = judgment; everything else has a cited source. Raw data is in `cdl-2026-10-28/moat/`. This builds on the differentiation, pricing, build-spec and UX reports from today.*

## 0. Bottom line

- **The table-stakes claims are crowded.** 8 of 11 iOS rivals sampled say "all 50 states" in their descriptions. Every major rival lists all six endorsements. Hanh Le already has Spanish and an "Unlimited AI Tutor". Solvely and CDL Pass (Google Play) already have audio. DMV Genie already has a pass guarantee. Claiming any of these in itself is not a moat.
- **What nobody shows is exactness and upkeep.** No sampled rival description mentions:
  - Texas's separate Commercial Rules test, which is taken first.
  - The three-choice answer format used in New York and Iowa.
  - Which states have moved to the AAMVA modernized manual.
  - Florida's and Texas's 2026 English-only knowledge tests.
  - A public record of fixes.

  Meanwhile, the rules keep moving:
  - 49 CFR Part 383 (CDL standards) had **8 amendment dates in 2026 so far**, and Part 172 (HazMat tables and placards) had **7** (eCFR versioner API).
  - PHMSA published HM-215R in Feb 2026 and HM-215S on Oct 27, 2026.

  A team that ships in days can keep 51 exact state formats current, with a public log. Two-person incumbents demonstrably don't: wrong-answer-key and "not on the test" complaints make up 33% of coded complaints (UX report).
- **Speed compounds most in the record, not the features.** The fix log, the miss-rate data, SEO page age and the review count all get better each month and can't be backfilled by a copier. Features themselves (audio, AI, Spanish) can be copied in weeks.
- **The ESL moat is weaker than assumed.**
  - Florida (Feb 6, 2026) and Texas (Jun 1, 2026) now test CDL knowledge in English only.
  - FMCSA's non-domiciled CDL rule (effective Mar 16, 2026) makes about 194k current holders ineligible over 5 years.
  - English-proficiency out-of-service enforcement is active.

  So reframe it as an "**English-test bridge**": learn in Spanish, pass in English. Don't build it as a Spanish-language test app.
- **Odds impact is honest but small.** Moats justify a CDL-specific hit rate of **~0.18 [J] (range 0.14–0.22)** against the queue default of 0.12. In the plan model (Scenario B, C replaces CAL), that moves base odds from **m12 67.1% to 67.5%** and **m18 81.3% to 81.5%**. One app is one draw of about 30. For the CDL app alone, median net profit at maturity goes from **$74 to $131/mo**, and P(≥$1k/mo net) from **11.8% to 17.8%**. Don't lift the hit rate for the rest of the queue on this evidence.

**Ship speed [E].**
- The state format table plus 51 state pages takes 6–8 CC-days, but human verification is the bottleneck: about 51 × 20 min ≈ 17 h of Sean or Lindsey checking against DMV sources.

## 3. Data and feedback moat

- **How fast things change.** eCFR distinct amendment dates by year:

  | Part | 2017–2025 | 2026 YTD | Last change |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 383 | about 1–7 per year | 8 | Jul 22, 2026 |
  | 172 | about 1–10 per year | 7 | Sep 8, 2026 |
  | 391 | — | 3 | — |
  | 393 | — | 4 | — |

  Add two PHMSA harmonization NPRMs in 2026 (HM-215R Feb 10; HM-215S Oct 27, comments due Dec 28, 2026), with a final rule likely in 2027 [E]. The modernized-manual rollout runs state by state through 2027. That works out to roughly a material change somewhere every 2–4 weeks [E].
- **Mechanics.** Each question stores source citations (CFR section or manual page). A nightly job diffs the eCFR versioner, the Federal Register API and DMV manual URLs, then flags every question citing a changed section. A fix goes out in the next build or an OTA update, and the log entry is public.
- **Legal line.** Collect topic chips and "harder or easier than the app" ratings only. Never ask for, store or publish actual test questions. Many states treat test content as secure, and the user agreement should say so. Miss-rate data must be opt-in and aggregated, with no PII.

## 4. Distribution moats

- **SEO.**
  - The 15-row Semrush batch adds about 4,650/mo across the state and endorsement terms (excluding the car and moto heads).
  - With Task 1's terms, the CDL long tail is about 12k/mo at KD ≤ 28 [E].
  - Spanish *search* demand is near zero: "cdl practice test spanish" shows 0 and "examen cdl en español" 50. Spanish demand lives on **YouTube**, not Google.
  - Risk: Google's scaled-content policy. Every page needs unique, sourced state facts.
- **Video.** Q&A-style CDL test videos regularly reach 30k–770k views, from single-person channels. Some can be generated from the bank: question cards, voice-over and an answer reveal. No paid spend is needed, and no outreach. YouTube's repetitive-content rules mean each video needs real explanation, not just a slideshow.
- **Partnerships.** Sales work in every case, so defer them. The one no-outreach version is a public "for instructors" page with a free classroom quiz link.

## 6. Brand and trust

- **Public accuracy log** from day 1. Every fix is dated and links the source and question ID.
- **Transparent changelog** in-app and on the web.
- **Reviews flywheel.** Prompt for a review *after* a passed mock and after the user reports passing the real test. Never prompt after a miss.
- **Timing [E].** At 80 sales/mo and about a 5% review rate, the app gets ~4 ratings a month, so ~50 ratings takes about a year. Speed can't shortcut this, which is why the log and SEO must carry trust early.
- **Examiner review.** Optional one-off purchase, after profit, with Sean's approval.

## 7. Where speed wins and where it doesn't

- **Week 1 post-MVP:** change-watch and fix log, cited explanations, TX Commercial Rules bank, format table for the top 10 states.
- **Month 1:** all 51 format rows and state pages, all endorsements plus renewal and L/Z, the video pipeline, test-day check-in.
- **Month 3:** miss-rate data, ES bridge, pre-trip audio, Cab Mode, motorcycle if the kill line has been cleared.
- **Speed doesn't help with:**
  - App Review (1–3 days per build).
  - The rating count (about a year to 50).
  - Domain and page age (SEO typically takes 3–9 months [E]).
  - Trust.
  - Human verification of state facts.

**Why 0.18 and not higher [J].**

- Against: 8 of 11 rivals already claim 50 states; Hanh Le is polished and has AI and Spanish; English-only testing and the non-domiciled rule shrink the immigrant segment; ratings take about a year.

**Bottom-up check [E].** At an average ticket of about $22 (only an estimate) and Apple's 15% small-business rate:
- The 80-sales target is about $1.5k/mo net.
- The 25-sales kill line is about $470/mo.
- $1k/mo net needs about 54 sales/mo.
